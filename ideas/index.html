Heart Ideas

Seventeen themed hearts from CVS Health's public merchandise store, The Heart Collection, plus the plain red original. Each one is a lapel pin for one of CVS's employee groups.

They're grouped by how each heart uses color and pattern. Each group is tagged with the All-Star Studio star style that works the same way.

Stripes and bands

Star style: bands

Parallel stripes are the most direct way to say “these parts belong together.” They read clearly even at pin size.

Rays from a center

Star style: rays

Lines that fan out from one point give a sense of energy and of everything meeting in the middle.

Quarters and facets

Star style: facets

Dividing the shape into a few large pieces gives each color an equal, unmistakable share.

Geometric mosaic

Star style: mosaic

Small tiles let many shapes and shades coexist. This is closest to the planned mosaic style.

Repeating motifs

Later idea

A small pattern repeated across the heart. All-Star Studio has dots and diagonal stripes for now; richer motifs are a later idea.

Gradients and soft shapes

Later idea

Smooth color blends and organic overlaps feel calm and modern. Gradients are on the later list for All-Star Studio.

Pictures

Later idea

A figure or object tells the story directly. The rest of the heart becomes background.

What the set has in common

  • One shape, never altered. Every heart uses the identical angular outline with a silver border. All the variety happens inside. All-Star Studio follows the same rule with its one star shape.
  • Most use 2 to 5 colors. PRIDE+ is the exception, and it stays readable because every stripe runs the same direction.
  • One idea per heart. Each heart commits to a single technique (stripes, rays, tiles, a gradient, a picture) instead of mixing several.
  • Imagery over a legend. Most of these tell their story with a picture or a feeling, not one color per value. All-Star Studio's plan is closer to PRIDE+ or aNative, where each color stands for something specific.

Questions to talk through

  1. Which of these hearts catch your eye first, and what do you like about them?
  2. Should the star read as a set of separate meanings (like stripes or quarters), or as one blended feeling (like a gradient)?
  3. How many colors feel right: 3 or 4 bold ones, or 6 or more?
  4. Does the contest say anything about shape, colors, outlines, or file format?
  5. Is a small picture or symbol inside the star worth exploring later?
